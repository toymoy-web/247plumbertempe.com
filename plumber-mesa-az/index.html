Geography

Tempe's Largest Neighbor To The East

Mesa is the largest city in the East Valley, with about 504,000 residents in the 2020 census, and it borders Tempe along the east side. From downtown Tempe the drive into central Mesa is short, which makes Mesa one of our most natural dispatch areas. Our separate West Mesa page covers the Dobson Ranch side, and this page covers the rest of the city.

The original Mesa townsite was settled in the late 1870s, and the blocks around downtown Mesa and the Main Street light rail corridor hold some of the oldest homes in the city. Moving east and southeast, the housing gets steadily newer, with subdivisions from the 1980s through the 2000s. A single Mesa call can mean a 1950s ranch house with original supply lines or a 2005 home with a first-generation water heater that is now due.

Like Tempe, Mesa draws mainly on Salt River Project and Central Arizona Project water, so hard water scale is a regional constant here. Water heaters, dishwashers, and faucet cartridges wear out faster than national averages suggest. A six-month water heater flush is a sensible default in Mesa for the same reason it is in Tempe.

We cover every Mesa ZIP code outside the west-side ZIPs on our West Mesa page, including 85204, 85205, 85206, and 85210, and every job gets the same flat, confirmed-before-we-start pricing. Water heater repair and drain cleaning are the two services we are asked about most across those ZIPs, with sewer camera inspections close behind for buyers of older homes.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Mesa

Shut Off The Main Valve

If you know where your main water shutoff is, turning it off immediately stops the flow while help is on the way.

Kill Power To Wet Areas

If water is near an outlet, panel, or plugged-in appliance, shutting off power to that area at the breaker is a real safety step.

Clear The Immediate Area

Move furniture, electronics, and belongings out of the affected space if it's safe to do so, to limit further damage.
