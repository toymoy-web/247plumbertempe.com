Tankless Water Heater Service in Tempe, AZ

A tankless unit in Tempe needs a different maintenance rhythm than the national average, because our water is genuinely harder than most places.

Tankless water heater service covers descaling, flow restoration, and error-code diagnosis for a unit that's stopped performing the way it did when it was new. Unlike a tank unit, a tankless heater doesn't have a bottom for sediment to settle in, but it does have a narrow heat exchanger, and that's exactly where Tempe's hard water leaves its mark.

According to the City of Tempe's own water quality data, hardness here averages 15 grains per gallon. That's enough mineral load that a tankless unit's manufacturer-recommended descaling interval, often annual in moderate water, should really be treated as a six-month interval here without a whole-home softener in place.

Common Problems

What Tankless Units Actually Need

Reduced Hot Water Flow

Scale narrowing the heat exchanger passages is the most common cause of flow that's noticeably weaker than when the unit was new.

Error Codes

Most tankless error codes trace back to either scale buildup, a venting issue, or a gas supply problem, each with a different fix.

Inconsistent Temperature

A unit that can't maintain steady temperature under demand is often struggling against scale rather than an electronic fault.

Unit Not Igniting

Ignition failures on gas units are sometimes scale-related and sometimes a separate component issue, which is why diagnosis comes before any part gets replaced.

The Descaling Process

How Service Actually Works

Isolate

Service valves isolate the unit so a descaling solution can circulate without affecting the rest of the plumbing system.

Circulate

A cleaning solution, typically a food-safe descaler, circulates through the heat exchanger to dissolve mineral buildup.

Flush

The unit is flushed clean of both scale and the descaling solution before being put back into service.

Test

Flow rate and temperature consistency are tested under real demand before the job is called done.

No Service Valves Yet?

Older tankless installs sometimes skip the isolation valves that make descaling straightforward; we can add them during the same visit so future service is faster.

Local Context

Where This Comes Up In Tempe

Tankless units show up in newer condo and apartment buildings near Tempe Town Lake and in remodeled homes across South Tempe. Tempe's hard water scales their heat exchangers quickly.

A six-month descaling visit is a sensible rhythm for these households, and we add isolation valves on older installs that skipped them.
