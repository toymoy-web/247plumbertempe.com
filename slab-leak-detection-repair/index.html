Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Tempe, AZ

Tempe's expansive clay and caliche soil is a real, physical reason slab leaks happen here more than in cities built on more stable ground.

A slab leak is a leak in a water line running underneath a home's concrete foundation, and in Tempe that's a genuinely common call, not a rare one. Much of the city sits on expansive clay and caliche soil that swells when it's wet and pulls back tight when it dries out, and that repeated shifting is exactly the kind of stress that eventually cracks a copper or galvanized line laid decades ago.

South Tempe homes built mainly from the 1970s through the 1990s are the most common source of these calls. Original copper lines from that era are now old enough, and Tempe's hard water has been corroding them from the inside long enough, that a slab leak becomes a real possibility rather than a remote one.

Local Causes

Why Slab Leaks Happen in Tempe

Expansive Clay & Caliche

Soil that swells and shrinks with moisture puts real, repeated stress on a rigid line underneath the slab.

Aging Copper Lines

Original copper plumbing in 1970s-1990s South Tempe homes is old enough to be at real risk of pinhole leaks.

Hard Water Corrosion

Tempe's mineral-heavy water accelerates internal corrosion in copper lines over decades.

Ground Temperature Swings

Repeated heating and cooling of the slab itself adds another source of stress on the pipe beneath it.

Signs to Watch For

How a Slab Leak Actually Shows Up

Warm Spot On The Floor

A hot-water line leak under the slab often shows up as an unexplained warm patch on tile or flooring.

Rising Water Bill

A slab leak can run continuously, which shows up as a steady, unexplained increase in usage.

Low Water Pressure

A significant slab leak can noticeably drop pressure at fixtures throughout the house.

Sound Of Running Water

Water audible under the floor with every fixture off is one of the more obvious signs, though not every slab leak makes noise.

Decision Framework

Direct Repair vs. Rerouting the Line

Direct Slab Repair

  • Repairs the exact damaged section of pipe
  • Requires cutting and patching a section of the slab
  • Often the faster option for a single, accessible leak point
  • Doesn't address the age of the rest of the line

Reroute Above Slab

  • Abandons the damaged section entirely, running new line through walls or the attic
  • Avoids cutting the slab at all
  • A stronger option when the existing line is old enough that more leaks are likely
  • More labor to route the new line, but reduces the odds of a repeat call
