Pool leak detection covers a real, common need in Tempe specifically: according to a Realtor.com analysis of property records, roughly 46 percent of Tempe single-family homes have a backyard pool, one of the highest rates of any city in the country. That density means pool plumbing leaks, distinct from a house's own water lines, are a genuine, recurring service category here rather than a rare specialty.

The first real diagnostic step is ruling out evaporation, which is often mistaken for a leak in Arizona's dry heat. A simple bucket test, comparing water loss inside a bucket against the pool's actual water level over 24 hours, separates a real leak from expected evaporation before any repair work starts.

Common Leak Sources

Where Pool Leaks Actually Happen

Skimmer Line Cracks

Expansive clay and caliche soil movement can crack the plumbing lines running from the pool to the skimmer and equipment pad.

Equipment Pad Fittings

Pump, filter, and heater connections are common points for a slow leak to develop at a fitting or seal.

Pool Shell Cracks

Ground movement can crack the pool shell itself, a different and often more involved repair than a plumbing line leak.

Spa Jets & Light Fixtures

Fittings around spa jets and pool lights are common, often overlooked leak points.

Detection Method

How We Find the Actual Leak

Bucket Test First

Confirms real water loss beyond normal evaporation before committing to a full leak search.

Pressure Testing

Isolating and pressure-testing plumbing lines narrows down whether the leak is in the pipe versus the shell or equipment.

Dye Testing

A dye test at suspected fitting or crack locations can visually confirm the exact leak point underwater.

Equipment Inspection

Pump, filter, and heater connections are checked directly, since equipment-pad leaks are a common and straightforward-to-fix cause.
