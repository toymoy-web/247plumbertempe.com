Whole-Home Repiping in Tempe, AZ

Repiping replaces a home's water supply lines entirely rather than patching each leak as it happens. It's the right conversation once a home has had more than one unrelated leak, since that pattern is a real signal the whole line, not just one section, has reached the end of its service life.

This comes up most in South Scottsdale's mid-century homes, where original galvanized steel supply lines from the 1940s through the 1960s are old enough that internal corrosion is often widespread. It also shows up in older South Tempe and Guadalupe homes with original 1970s-90s copper that Tempe's hard water has been corroding for decades.

Signs You Need It

When Repiping Makes More Sense Than Repeated Repairs

Multiple Unrelated Leaks

A second or third leak in a different part of the same line is a sign of widespread corrosion, not bad luck.

Low Water Pressure Throughout

Corrosion narrowing pipe diameter across the whole system, not just one fixture, points to the line itself rather than a single fixture problem.

Discolored Water At Multiple Taps

Rust-colored water from more than one fixture suggests the supply line itself is corroding, not just one water heater or valve.

Original Galvanized Pipe

A home confirmed to still have original galvanized supply lines is a strong candidate regardless of whether it's leaking yet.

Materials

PEX vs. Copper for a Repipe

PEX Flexibility

PEX tubing snakes through existing wall cavities with less demolition than rigid copper, which often means a faster job with less patching.

Copper Durability

Copper has a long track record and is preferred in some applications, though it costs more and requires more invasive installation.

Hard Water Resistance

Both materials handle Tempe's hard water better than aging galvanized steel, which is the material actually failing in most repipe calls.

Code Compliance

Whichever material is used, the installation is done according to the City of Tempe's own adopted plumbing code, with permits and inspection.

Process

How a Repipe Actually Works

1

Assess

We confirm the existing pipe material and map out the full supply line before recommending a repipe.

2

Plan Access

We plan the route to minimize wall and ceiling openings, patched afterward.

3

Repipe

New lines are run and connected, with the old line abandoned or removed depending on access.

4

Test & Permit

The new system is pressure-tested and inspected per the City of Tempe's permit process.
