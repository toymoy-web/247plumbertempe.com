Emergency Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Tempe, AZ

A sump pump in Tempe earns its keep during monsoon season, and a failed one at the wrong moment means water where it shouldn't be.

Sump pump service covers installation for a home with a basement, crawlspace, or low-lying drainage point, plus repair when an existing pump fails to keep up. In most of the Valley this is a lower-volume need than in basement-heavy climates, but homes with below-grade areas or persistent drainage issues near the foundation still depend on a working pump, especially during monsoon season.

Tempe's monsoon season, roughly mid-June through the end of September, brings sudden, heavy storms that can overwhelm drainage faster than a slow seasonal rain would. A sump pump that hasn't been tested before that season starts is a real, avoidable risk.

Common Problems

What We Fix

Pump Not Activating

A float switch that's stuck or a pump that's simply failed won't activate when water rises, defeating the entire point of having one.

Pump Running Constantly

A pump that never shuts off is often a sign of a stuck switch or a drainage issue feeding more water than expected.

Insufficient Capacity

An undersized pump for the actual water volume during a heavy monsoon storm can be overwhelmed even while technically working.

No Backup Power

A pump with no battery backup stops working during a power outage, which is exactly when monsoon storms are most likely to knock out power.

Installation

Getting It Right The First Time

Proper Sizing

Pump capacity is matched to the actual drainage volume for the specific property, not a generic default.

Battery Backup

A battery backup system keeps the pump running during a power outage, which matters most during the exact storms that cause flooding.

Discharge Line Routing

The discharge line is routed to carry water safely away from the foundation, not just out the nearest wall.

Alarm Systems

A high-water alarm alerts you if the pump can't keep up, giving you a chance to respond before real damage occurs.

Local Context

Where This Comes Up In Tempe

Tempe has few basements, so most sump pump calls come from low spots near the foundation, below-grade garages, and drainage problems along canals and the Salt River channel.

Low-lying areas near Tempe Town Lake and Rio Salado Parkway are the first places to check before monsoon season, and a tested pump with battery backup is cheap insurance.
