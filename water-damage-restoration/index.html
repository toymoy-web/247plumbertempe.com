Emergency Water Damage Restoration in Tempe, AZ

When a plumbing failure floods part of a Tempe home, the plumbing repair and the water damage cleanup are two different jobs, and both need to start fast.

Water damage restoration covers what happens to a home after a burst pipe, a slab leak, a failed water heater, or a sewer backup has already put water where it doesn't belong. That's a distinct job from the plumbing repair itself: fixing the pipe stops the water, but the drying, cleanup, and structural assessment that follow are their own process, with their own timeline and their own risks if they're delayed.

The biggest risk on a delay is mold. Wet drywall, subfloor, and carpet padding in Tempe's summer heat and humidity swings can develop mold growth within 24 to 48 hours, according to the drying-time standards the restoration industry works from. That window is why restoration starts the same day as the plumbing fix, not after.

Common Causes

What Leads to a Restoration Call

Burst Pipe Flooding

A burst pipe, especially one that runs overnight or while a home is empty, can soak drywall, flooring, and subfloor before anyone finds it.

Slab Leak Damage

A slab leak that's gone unnoticed for weeks can wick moisture up into baseboards and flooring well beyond the leak's actual location.

Water Heater Failure

A failed tank can release its full contents at once, often in a garage or utility closet with limited drainage.

Sewer Backup

A sewer backup brings contaminated water into the equation, which changes both the cleanup protocol and the materials that can be safely dried versus need replacing.

The Restoration Process

What Actually Happens

Assessment & Documentation

We document the extent of the damage with photos and moisture readings before any cleanup starts, which matters for your insurance claim.

Water Extraction

Standing water is removed first, using pumps or extraction equipment sized to the actual volume involved.

Structural Drying

Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure itself, not just the visible surface, tracked with moisture meters until readings return to normal.

Mold Prevention

Materials that stayed wet long enough to risk mold get treated or, when saturation is too deep, removed and replaced rather than gambled on.
