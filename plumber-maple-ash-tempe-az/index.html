Neighborhood Overview

About Maple-Ash

Maple-Ash sits just south of downtown Tempe. It runs south from University Drive, with Mill Avenue along its western side. Published descriptions differ a little on the other edges, so we describe the area by those anchor streets and treat the rest as approximate.

It is widely described as one of Tempe's oldest surviving neighborhoods, with some homes dating to the 1910s. Real estate descriptions mention old bungalows beside newer condominiums and remodeled properties, so a single block can hold several eras of work. A 2014 KJZZ report noted the neighborhood on the Arizona Preservation Foundation's list of most endangered historic places.

That mix is why we treat Maple-Ash calls as inspections first. We look at what is actually installed, explain it in plain language, and confirm the price in writing before any work begins.

Older Neighborhood Plumbing

What Matters In An Older Neighborhood

Identify Before You Assume

Pipe material and condition vary from one house to the next, especially where a home has been added onto or remodeled. We confirm what is installed rather than guessing from the year a house was built.

Camera First For Sewer Lines

A camera inspection shows the real condition of a sewer line, including roots and offsets, without digging. It is also worth considering before you buy an older home.

Mature Trees And Roots

Mature trees are part of what makes older Tempe streets pleasant. Roots are a recognized cause of sewer line blockages in older neighborhoods generally, so a drain that keeps slowing down deserves a camera look.

Mixed Eras On One Street

Where a bungalow has been updated, newer plumbing can connect to older lines. We check those connections, because they are a natural place to look when something starts to leak.
