Backflow Testing & Prevention in Tempe, AZ

The City of Tempe requires annual backflow testing for most properties with a prevention assembly, and it's the property owner's responsibility to keep it current.

A backflow prevention assembly stops contaminated water from being pulled backward into the clean water supply, a real risk anywhere a property connects to irrigation, a pool, or certain commercial equipment. According to the City of Tempe's own backflow prevention program under City Code Chapter 37, most properties with an assembly are required to have it tested annually, with the property owner responsible for scheduling that test and submitting results.

The City typically sends a notice of the test due date through the monthly utility bill about 60 days ahead of time, but the responsibility to actually get it tested and keep the assembly in working order sits with the property owner, not the City.

Who Needs This

When a Backflow Assembly Is Required

Irrigation Systems

Properties with an irrigation system connection are a common category requiring backflow protection and annual testing.

Pools

Given how common pools are in Tempe, pool-related connections are another frequent category requiring a tested assembly.

Commercial Properties

Restaurants and other commercial properties with certain equipment connections often require higher-hazard reduced pressure zone assemblies.

Fire Sprinkler Systems

Properties with fire sprinkler systems have their own backflow testing requirement, including a full flow test performed by a certified tester.

What Testing Involves

The Annual Test Process

Certified Testing

Testing is performed with calibrated equipment by a certified tester, checking that the assembly's valves are functioning correctly.

Results Submitted To The City

Test results are submitted to the City as part of the compliance requirement, not just kept on file privately.

Repairs If Needed

An assembly that fails testing needs repair or replacement before it's back in compliant, satisfactory condition.

Installation & Permits

New assembly installations require a permit through Tempe's Building Safety Division, which we handle.
